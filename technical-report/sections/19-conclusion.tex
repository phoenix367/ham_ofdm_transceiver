\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The project set out to reproduce a published amateur-radio OFDM PHY
layer and ended with two boards that are radios.  The reproduction
itself is closed: 44/44 worked examples bit-exact, the BER/PER figures
reproduced, and the sensitivity gap traced to three specific
synchronization defects and eliminated to within 0.3\,dB of a
genie-synchronized bound.  On that foundation the waveform was
extended an order of magnitude in each direction --- down to
$-17.9$\,dB and 7.8\,bit/s (78\,\% of Shannon capacity at $-20$\,dB)
and up to $+4.7$\,dB and 1059\,bit/s with 16-QAM --- and wrapped in a
link layer that measures everything it can observe and feeds all of it
back: receiver-driven rate adaptation over a 13-rung measured ladder,
CRC-gated HARQ from exported LLRs, learned per-rung sensitivity
offsets, closed-loop AFC netting, streamed selective-repeat bursts, a
capability handshake, and a broadcast mode that adapts between blocks
on a statistics reply the receiver only sends when it can hear.

The model was then implemented twice.  The fixed-point twin matched
and then beat the floating-point chain at the sensitivity edge, and its
two-lag unwrap recovered a defect worth 8\,\% of all acquisitions ---
an RTL-oriented discipline producing the better algorithm.  The C
implementation, verified bit for bit against that twin, was brought
inside an STM32H743 by measurement rather than projection: a receiver
whose memory follows the signal (561\,KB), an acquisition that keeps
up (9.5\,\% of the core), and a front end that is DC-blind by
construction.  Two of those boards, joined by a wire, then carried a
68\,kB file at 87\,\% of the raw channel rate, a 73\,kB file by
broadcast without a lost frame, AX.25 and TCP through host bridges,
and push-to-talk speech at 250\,bit/s decoded as it arrived; an SDR
put the same stack on a 7\,MHz carrier and recovered frames off the
air.

Three results deserve emphasis beyond the feature list.  First, the
measured LLR recalibration --- 1.5--2\,dB from correcting the
\emph{shape} of the demodulator's confidence, at zero over-the-air
cost --- illustrates how much sensitivity can hide between a correct
demodulator and a correct decoder.  Second, the boards changed what
the report is about: every defect the stand found was in a time
constant, an ownership boundary, or a policy that had been correct for
the traffic it was written for, and none was in the DSP that the
simulations had spent a year on.  Third, a mode that cannot
retransmit turned out to be the instrument that measures the
receiver.  Acknowledged modes had been hiding an 8\,\% acquisition
defect and a commit-rule defect on a clean wire; broadcast, and then
voice, exposed both, and the second cost two wrong explanations that
each survived a round of measurement before a deterministic
reproduction on the host settled it.  The methodological lesson is
cheap and was learned expensively: interleave the arms, keep the raw
artefact, and refuse ``statistics'' as an explanation until the
statistic survives an attempt to reproduce it.

Everything claimed here is regenerable from the repository.  Every
channel a figure was measured on is defined in one place; the
regression suites are self-verifying in three languages; every
sensitivity figure has its sweep script and JSON dump; every board
finding has a bench target that reproduces it without the board; and
the demonstrations are recordings any copy of the receiver decodes
blind.  The step that remains is the one the wire cannot take and the
SDR has been readied for: the same frames over an ionospheric path.
